**Luxor Capital Group (his firm)**
- Founded October 2002 by Christian Leone, ex-Goldman Sachs; "principally owned by Christian Leone" [11]. Regulatory AUM was **$5,037,229,000** at 12/31/2025, across 37 accounts (32 pooled vehicles, about $4.94B), with 39 employees (ADV Part 1 amended 8/25/2026) [12][13].
- Strategy in its own words: *"a fundamentally driven and opportunistic approach using multiple strategies to invest across the capital structures of U.S. and non-U.S. companies"* [11]. Situations include *"event driven, value equity, credit arbitrage, structural arbitrage, relative value, distressed, commodity related, activist and special situation. Underlying this opportunistic approach is a commitment to preserving capital and to uncovering market inefficiencies."* [11]
- *"...it will not allocate capital to situations where it believes that a positive return can be generated only if certain broader market conditions prevail."* [11] In other words, idiosyncratic alpha, not market beta.
- Long book: buys *"when it believes that such securities are trading notably below their intrinsic value as determined by Luxor through fundamental analysis of the underlying business... without regard to such companies' market capitalizations, geographic locations or market sectors."* [11]
- Short and hedge triggers include *"negative tangible book value; temporary overvaluation due to short-term market euphoria for a sector; a faulty business model; poor earnings; questionable accounting practices; deteriorating fundamentals; or weak management unable to adapt..."* [11]. Expect him to probe these risks in a long pitch.
- Also runs a private and pre-IPO "Closed-End Approach", plus affiliated advisers: Lugard Road (internet, software, consumer, tech), and GGK, HNG and Thebes GPs [11][25].
- Marketplace thesis from the 2023 Ritchie Bros. proxy fight: *"a long and successful track record of investing in marketplace businesses globally over our 20 plus year history. We typically look to invest in companies where we see multi-year compounding opportunities. We are not typically involved as 'activists', but consider ourselves engaged shareholders."* [19] Also: *"For network effect driven businesses like RBA and IAA, value is created by driving liquidity in the network with more sellers and more buyers."* [19]
- Leone in 2009: *"...the willingness to move across asset classes and geographies when appropriate shaped the way Luxor has approached to market"* [24]. The firm was then described as "opportunistic, event-oriented credit" doing "bottom-up, security-level research" [24].
- Track record is volatile. The fund lost 19.2% in 2015 and put 12% of assets (including Delivery Hero and Foodpanda) into a side-pocket SPV in 2016. Consultant Cliffwater blamed "poor investment selection and inadequate hedging" [22][23]. Per Insider Monkey, it gained 70%/35%/53%/68% in 2017–2020, fell about 35% in 1H22, and was up about 60% through 9M23 [21]. *These figures are secondary and unaudited.* Lesson for us: expect him to care about liquidity, hedging, and drawdowns.

## Implications for our pitch

1. **Frame it like a hedge-fund stock pitch.** Cover the variant perception (what the market misprices and why), a dated catalyst, a valuation bridge to the target, base/bull/bear cases, and the key risk that kills the thesis. This matches Luxor's "misvalued... intrinsic value... catalyst/event" process [11].
2. **Show we understand risk without shorts or derivatives.** Luxor hedges with index puts [14]; we can't. Address beta, correlation to SPX/IWM, liquidity, position size, and a hard stop-loss. A thesis that "only works if the market goes up" runs against Luxor's stated rule [11].
3. **Good-fit idea types (inference):**
   - **(a)** A mispriced global marketplace or platform, perhaps non-US, with network-effect economics: take rate, liquidity, cohort data.
   - **(b)** A mid-cap special situation: spin-off, post-merger re-rating, capital-allocation change, or "orphaned" asset owner.
   - **(c)** Real-asset or infrastructure cash flows such as leasing, LNG or power, with a clear free-cash-flow yield story.
   - **(d)** For fixed income: a stressed or convertible credit where we can explain the capital structure. Luxor trades "across the capital structure" [11].
4. **Avoid simply repeating Luxor's holdings.** Pitching NRG, FTAI, AER, GLNG or Somnigroup gets expert-level pushback, and he may already hold the opposite view on names Luxor sold (BDX, Harrow, TSMC common). If we do pitch one, bring differentiated work.
5. **For non-US names, be exact about details:** listing venue, ADR vs local shares, FX exposure in USD terms, governance, and liquidity. He has EM/LatAm and international-studies training [2], and the competition rules call these details out [7]. A Mexico or LatAm idea would meet an informed judge. *That is an inference; bring real local detail.*
6. **Capital allocation and M&A discipline matter to this shop** [19]. Include a slide on management incentives, buybacks and M&A track record.
7. **Data or alt-data support** (app downloads, GMV trackers, pricing scrapes) fits a marketplace-style diligence process and helps for the "best data-driven" prize [7].
