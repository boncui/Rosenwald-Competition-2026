## TL;DR for pitching

- **He is a Graham-and-Dodd, margin-of-safety value investor.** Dalton's stated first priority is "capital preservation" [9]. Historically he wanted a new position at roughly a 50% discount to fair value, and EV/EBITDA is the metric the firm uses most [6]. The course he teaches centers on buying assets at "50 cents on the dollar" [7][57]. Pitches that depend on momentum or narrative alone will not land. Show a quantified discount to intrinsic or private-market value.
- **He rewards a catalyst plus an owner-aligned management team.** He wants managers who "think like owner/managers," have "skin in the game," are "long-term greedy," and who buy back stock [6][30]. His year-end 2025 letter pushes buybacks of 5-10% a year, executives owning 3-5x their fixed pay in stock, and majority-independent boards [30]. He likes spin-offs, MBOs, take-private or private-market bids, and governance reform as the path to closing a discount [28][30][32].
- **His home turf is Japan, with Korea second.** He has bought Japanese shares since 1972 [8]. His Dec 2025 letter says "Japan's capital markets are entering a new golden era" [30]. He is Japan's most prolific activist (40 companies over 3 years) [46][47]. Dalton opened a Seoul office in 2025 and is pushing a Korea "value-up" thesis [53][55]. A pitch that is cheap on P/B or EV/EBITDA, cash-rich, and backed by a governance catalyst in Japan or Korea fits his playbook exactly (inference).
- **He is global and contrarian.** The syllabus covers "all asset classes and geographies": China, India, SE Asia, LatAm, Africa, Europe, real estate, special situations, and credit [57]. He was early to Korea (1992), Russia (1996) and South Africa [5][6]. Contrarian ideas are welcome if you show the institutional and FX work, which competition rules also require for non-US assets [58].
- **Dalton's US-listed book leans on India-linked IT services and AI hardware** (NVIDIA, MakeMyTrip, Cognizant, Genpact, TaskUs) [62]. A June 2026 Dalton note is bullish on Asian "owner-operator" AI hardware suppliers such as Accton, Delta, SK Hynix and DISCO [56]. AI is acceptable to him when framed as owner-operators trading at reasonable multiples (inference).
- **He punishes value traps, weak governance and "zombies."** The syllabus contrasts "Cannibals vs. Zombies" (buyback compounders vs. capital hoarders) and covers value traps explicitly [57]. Address why the discount will close.
- **The final deliverable in his own class is an individual investment pitch.** The student fund he funds holds positions for years (Jefferies since 2012, CK Hutchison since 2013) [57][60]. Frame a 3-5 year thesis, not a trade (inference from the fund's practice).
- **Avoid:** thin downside analysis, leverage-dependent stories, and unfamiliar-market pitches without legal and market-structure detail. He also dismisses short-termist activism: "doing it wrong" [8].

## Investment philosophy & style

**Core value discipline**
- "Capital preservation is our primary concern." Dalton seeks to "invest with a margin of safety" and to "opportunistically seek to identify excellent undervalued assets worldwide" [9].
- New positions typically sit at a discount of 50% or more to fair value. In 2013 the whole portfolio sat at about a 57% discount. EV/EBITDA is the most common valuation tool [6].
- He meets management before a position goes to full size and holds a starter position (about 0.5%) until then [6].

**Alignment and owner-operators**
- "Having management thinking like owner/managers is the best way for the long term to get the power of compounding working on your side as an investor." [6]
- His alignment checklist: skin in the game, access to the top of the corporate pyramid, "long-term greedy" managers who live on dividends and want to beat the competition [6].
- The Alliance Witan sleeve targets owner-operated small/mid caps with good governance, strong employees and entrepreneurial culture, and avoids value traps [27]. The UCITS fund targets owner-operated businesses above $0.5B market cap with aligned interests [22].

**Activism and governance, Japan**
- 2015: governance reform should mean investing excess capital well, not only paying it out. Short-sighted activists are "doing it wrong" [8].
- Year-end letter, Dec 2025 [30]:
  - "Japan's capital markets are entering a new golden era."
  - "Having invested in Japan for half a century, I can say that there has never been a more exciting time than today."
  - "most companies are easily capable of pursuing wage increases and growth investments, while also conducting annual share buybacks of 5–10%"
  - On EV/EBITDA, a company's own shares are often a better investment than acquisition targets.
  - Endorses METI's "Best Owner" concept.
  - "management has nowhere to hide"
  - MBOs and spin-offs should be considered "without preconceived conclusions"
  - Executives should hold 3-5x fixed pay in equity via restricted stock.
  - At least half the board should be independent, with capital-markets expertise ("not accountants, tax advisers, or commercial bankers").
  - Dalton will offer a board nominee where it owns 5% or more.
- Alliance Witan Japan update, Sep 2025 [28]:
  - "the Japanese market remains cheap globally"
  - "the Japanese rally is finally here. It is just beginning"
  - "The rest of our portfolio will likely receive bids over time... we focus on companies that are trading at significant discounts to what private market values are."
- Japan Times, May 2025 [32][33]:
  - "The government understands the Japanese stock market is really cheap."
  - "All aspects of government have been friends to us as shareholders for the first time in 30 years."
  - "I would love to negotiate a settlement."
  - This is a settlement-first, constructive tone.
- Ezaki Glico, Oct 2025 video [25]:
  - Dalton owns more than 10% of the roughly $2B company, which "could easily be worth double or triple this if consolidated with a global player."
  - Dalton clients "can handle some illiquidity."
  - This shows tolerance for illiquid, concentrated positions.
- Toyota Industries, May 2025: he called Akio Toyoda's buyout "gutsy." "Shareholders were at the bottom of the totem pole... What Toyoda is doing is turning that totem pole upside down" [35]. Dalton then sold out by Sep 2025 [28]. Diligent later described Dalton as opposing the privatization terms [47].

**Korea**
- Feb 2025: "Korea is a market with great potential... opportunities to enhance corporate value through improved governance, better capital allocation, and shareholder returns" [53].
- Nov 2023 paper [54]:
  - Korean holding companies trade at record discounts to NAV.
  - The KRW is cheap versus PPP.
  - Shareholder returns are rising.
  - A possible MSCI developed-market upgrade and K-culture tailwinds.
- Mar-Jun 2026 [55]:
  - KOSPI at 5,642 (25 Mar 2026), more than double a year earlier and above President Lee's 5,000 target.
  - About 1,500 Korean stocks trade below 1x P/B, and nearly a third of the market below 0.6x.
  - Dalton is "monitoring with excitement" Japan-style reforms: limits on duplicate listings, a push on low P/B, and KOSDAQ tiering.

**AI and technology**
- Jun 2026 note, "Asian owner-operators and the AI inflection point" (Owens Huang; photographed with Rosenwald and Accton founder AJ Huang) [56]:
  - "global investors continue to be underexposed to the slightly smaller family and entrepreneur-owned Asian companies which are similarly exposed to the AI, Datacenter and Digitalization trend."
  - Names Accton, Delta Electronics, SK Hynix/SK Square and DISCO.

**Teaching emphasis** (Fall 2026 syllabus [57])
- **Topics:**
  - Investing vs. speculating; contrarian thinking vs. groupthink.
  - Alignment of interests, competitive advantage, margin of safety.
  - Tangible book value and the balance sheet.
  - Value traps; "Cannibals vs. Zombies."
  - Geopolitics and FX; real estate.
  - Special situations: spin-offs, risk arbitrage, bankruptcies.
  - Credit and capital structure.
  - Country sessions: China, India, SE Asia, LatAm, Africa, Europe, and Japan activism.
- **Readings:** Buffett letters, Graham, Klarman's *Margin of Safety*, Greenblatt, Kroeber.
- **Final exam:** an individual investment pitch.

## Implications for our pitch

These are inferences drawn from the evidence above.

1. **Lead with the valuation gap and its measure.** State EV/EBITDA, P/B, net cash as a share of market cap, sum-of-the-parts or private-market value, and the percentage discount. He anchors on a discount of about 50% [6][20].
2. **Name the catalyst and who controls it.** Buybacks, spin-offs, take-privates, unwinding cross-holdings, index or exchange reforms (TSE P/B=1, Korea value-up). Explain why management is aligned or can be forced to be.
3. **A Japan or Korea governance-reform name is on-thesis but crowded with him.** Choosing one of Dalton's own holdings (e.g., Ezaki Glico, Fuji Media) risks sounding derivative, and he knows those companies better than anyone. Better options:
   - An adjacent, under-covered owner-operator.
   - A Korean holding-company discount.
   - A comparable reform story in another market.
   - If using a Dalton-adjacent name, bring a new angle.
4. **Do the institutional and FX homework.** Competition rules require USD returns and institutional detail for non-US assets [58]. The syllabus covers FX and geopolitics [57]. Cover the currency view (e.g., JPY/KRW cheap vs. PPP [54]), share classes, foreign ownership limits, and liquidity.
5. **Aim for long-horizon, owner-style theses.** Describe a 3-5 year compounding path with downside protection from the balance sheet. Avoid short-term trading arguments [8][60].
6. **Tech or AI ideas are acceptable if framed as value.** Examples: owner-operated Asian hardware suppliers at reasonable multiples [56], or India IT services and BPO names that look like AI losers but have aligned management (Dalton is adding Genpact, TaskUs and Cognizant [62]).
7. **Expect questions on value traps, capital allocation history, insider ownership and board independence.** Prepare a slide on each [30][57].
8. **Macro and fixed income prize:** his writing on JGB fiscal risk [51] and the KRW/JPY valuation view [54] are natural hooks. However, there is no evidence he personally favors bond pitches.
