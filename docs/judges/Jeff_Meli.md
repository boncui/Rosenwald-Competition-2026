## TL;DR for pitching to them

- **He is inside the competition, not just a guest judge.** He teaches *Managing Investment Funds* (FINC-GB 6020), the course that runs the ~$3mm Michael Price Student Investment Fund (MPSIF) [3][8]. **His current students score the first round** ("originality, clarity, and feasibility"), and the winning ideas can be put into MPSIF [7]. He was also on the 2025 final judging panel [7]. Write every page for a portfolio-management class that would actually have to hold the position.
- **Fixed income and market plumbing are his home ground.** He spent 25 years on corporate bonds, structured credit, liquidity, bond ETFs, portfolio trading, repo and the Treasury market, and on how bank capital rules change all of these [1][5][6]. He was once ranked a top-three investment-grade credit strategist by Institutional Investor [5]. A macro/FI pitch will face the hardest questions from him, and it can also earn the most credit from him.
- **Liquidity and implementation get checked.** His research measures transaction costs, how ETFs substitute for trading the underlying bonds, and how bond ETFs behave in stress [2][37][41]. If a pitch uses a bond ETF, expect questions on liquidity, premium/discount to NAV, what the fund actually holds, and how it trades in a sell-off.
- **Data-driven work should be tested against consensus.** He founded Barclays' Research Data Science initiative [5][39]. His undergraduate course has a CarMax case on "real-world use of alternative data to predict earnings", plus sessions on "importance of consensus" and "constructing a 'trade'" [11]. He will expect alternative data to predict something the consensus has wrong, with a backtest behind it.
- **Structural stories beat stories about the Fed.** He has argued many times that monetary policy is less powerful than people assume, and that deficits, labour supply, housing shortages and regulation drive outcomes [18][19][20]. Treat that as a debate stance, but it comes up again and again.
- **He punishes hype without cash flows.** Examples: doubting that "traditional asset pricing even applies to Bitcoin" [23]; saying AI names at "60 or 100 times" earnings are at "serious risk" if growth disappoints [17]; calling meme-stock episodes a "classic" bubble made worse by leverage [24]; and calling low measured volatility in private credit "more a question of optics" [21].
- **Say why your view is off-consensus, and give the bear case in full.** He built and hosted *The Flip Side*, a podcast where two analysts argue opposite sides [1][25]. The competition rules he works under ask for holding period, price targets, downside, stop-losses and institutional detail for unfamiliar assets [7].
- **Keep the valuation clean.** His course teaches how analysts "use (and abuse)" valuation concepts: the dividend discount model, EPS × P/E and how it relates to the DDM, buybacks versus dividends, and what drives multiples [11].

## Investment philosophy & style (with direct quotes, each cited)

**Caveat:** *The Flip Side* is a debate format in which "two Barclays Research analysts offer perspectives from different angles" [25]. Some positions below may be debate roles. The themes that also show up in his own research, op-eds and post-Barclays interviews carry the most weight: market structure, regulation and liquidity, and ETFs.

1. **Liquidity and transaction costs are part of returns.**
   - "Generally an asset that is illiquid will earn higher returns: investors have to be compensated for taking the risk that the asset can't be easily or cheaply, sold or managed. That has historically been an important source of returns in credit." [16]
   - "As the costs of trading go down, it becomes easier to take advantage of these opportunities because you're not paying away so much of the upside in transaction costs." [16]
2. **Benchmark-relative thinking.** "The goal of an active manager isn't necessarily total returns, it's really returns, relative to their benchmark. Beating the benchmark gets easier when trading is easier." [16]
3. **Systematic and quant methods add to fundamental credit work.**
   - "Factor and systematic investing... were limited to equities for a long time, but we now see them pick up momentum in credit." [16]
   - His JFI survey paper covers credit alpha signals (value, equity momentum, equity short interest, post-earnings-announcement drift) and "index inefficiencies, such as the overselling and subsequent recovery of fallen angels" [2].
   - "Not all investors look at the world through the same lens, and that means more opportunities for everyone." [16]
4. **Skeptical of long-standing valuation rules being bent to fit prices.** "It's always sort of a warning sign to me when we're forced to rethink decades-old valuation metrics just to make sense of current prices." (2021, on record-high IG bond prices) [22]
5. **Separates cash-flow assets from narrative assets.** "Tesla is a traditional financial asset. It's a stock. Its price is dependent on future cash flows... I'm not sure how traditional asset pricing even applies to Bitcoin." [23]
6. **Growth multiples need growth to show up.** "In order for a company to trade at 60 or 100 times its current earnings, that must mean that the expectations are for earnings to grow a lot and to grow fast... if any slowdown in the US economy reduces the appetite for investment today in AI, such that the growth required to justify these multiples never actually materializes, then those companies are at serious risk." (Aug 2024) [17]
7. **Wary of leverage and of safety that comes from official support.**
   - "There's a difference between safety that arises organically like from low interest rates or conservative management of balance sheets and safety that's provided by the official sector to a specific part of the economy." [22]
   - On private credit: "I think we can agree that the benefits of high leverage are all private." [21]
   - The low volatility of private credit is "more a question of optics than anything real about the value of the assets." [21]
8. **Monetary policy is less powerful than assumed; structural forces matter more.**
   - "I think the economy is evolving around the Fed and that interest rates are largely a sideshow." (Mar 2024) [18]
   - "The sensitivity of the economy to interest rates is far lower than we are conditioned to believe." (Sep 2023) [19]
9. **Bubbles and fads repeat.** "The retail angle here [is] just the most recent in the long history of fads or bubbles... every generation of investors needs to experience the high and the inevitable crash for themselves." [24]
10. **Research has to be good enough to pay for.** "Well-written, impactful fixed income research is valuable. And it is more valuable when it is not distributed widely." (2017) [42]
11. **Uses high-frequency data carefully.** "I think high frequency data can be tricky to interpret. There are structural factors, including the robust labor market..." (CNBC, Jan 2024) [29]

## Implications for our pitch

*Everything in this section is **inference** from the evidence above, not a stated preference of Meli's.*

**Idea types likely to resonate**
1. **A macro/FI idea based on market structure, run through liquid ETFs.** The rules ban derivatives, so express the view with Treasury, TIPS, IG, HY or fallen-angel ETFs.
   - Examples of theses in his area: Treasury supply and term premium under eSLR changes and a smaller Fed footprint; the post-regulatory liquidity premium in credit; bank-equity re-rating from capital relief [15][26][33].
   - Show you know how the ETF works: creation/redemption, NAV premium/discount history in March 2020, underlying liquidity, duration and spread exposure.
2. **A long-only systematic credit or fallen-angel idea.** His JFI paper cites fallen-angel overselling and recovery as an index inefficiency [2]. A backtested, rules-based fallen-angel ETF allocation (or an IG/HY value or momentum tilt) maps straight onto his research. Show transaction costs and capacity; he has written that costs eat alpha [16].
3. **A data-driven fundamental equity idea in the style of his CarMax case** [11]. Use alternative data (card spend, web traffic, job postings, NLP) to forecast a KPI or EPS, show where you differ from **consensus** and why the market hasn't priced it, and name the catalyst (earnings date). This fits the "best data-driven/quant" prize, which explicitly allows "creative use of alternative data" [7].
4. **Bank or financials equity with a regulatory catalyst.** This is his favourite subject [15], and his course includes a bank-equity/TBTF case [11]. The bar is high: you need to get CET1/SLR mechanics, ROE versus payout, and risk-adjusted returns right.
5. **Structural real-economy themes** (housing supply, labour supply, deficits) rather than "the Fed will cut, so buy X" [18][19].

**Framing**
- Open with a **one-sentence variant view versus consensus**, then add a **"Flip Side" slide**: the strongest bear case, argued seriously, and why you still hold the view.
- Include **everything the rubric asks for**: holding period, price target, downside, stop-loss, sizing, and USD/currency risk for non-US assets. Add institutional detail for anything unusual [7]. His students grade round one on "originality, clarity, and feasibility" [7].
- Show **implementation realism**: liquidity (ADV, bid-ask, ETF premium/discount), costs, and whether a ~$3mm student fund could actually hold it [3][7].
- **Valuation discipline**: tie multiples to growth, risk and payout (DDM ↔ P/E; buybacks versus dividends) [11]. Don't stretch a model to justify a price [22].
- Write clearly and briefly. His course has a session called "Power of the pen – Writing effective research" [11].

**What to avoid**
- Momentum or hype names with no cash-flow anchor (crypto-adjacent, meme stocks), and AI stories priced on hope without evidence of growth [17][23][24].
- Leveraged or inverse ETFs used casually. He distrusts leverage in the wrong hands [24], and the rules require their risks to be addressed [7].
- Pitches that depend on "the Fed will save it" or an implicit official put [22].
- Treating private-credit or illiquid-asset "low volatility" as real low risk [21].
- Mistakes in bond basics (duration versus spread, IG versus HY liquidity, how ETFs trade) — he will catch them.
