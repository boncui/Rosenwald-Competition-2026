## Do-not-analyze list

Every vector gets checked against this list before any research time goes into it. If it hits a **Hard** item, drop it. If it hits a **Soft** item, only keep it with a written reason.

Sources: [Competition_rules.md](Competition_rules.md), [Info.md](Info.md), and the judge profiles in [judges/](judges/).

### 1. Rule violations: Hard (disqualification)

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Short positions of any kind | Rules: "may not involve short positions" |
| Options, futures, swaps, warrants, CFDs, structured notes | Rules: no derivatives |
| Private companies, pre-IPO rounds, private credit funds, VC/PE funds | Must be public equity or fixed income, or a publicly traded ETF |
| Real estate, commodities, or crypto held **directly** (not through a listed security) | Outside "global public equity and fixed income" |
| Sanctioned or uninvestable securities (e.g., Russian listings), and markets a foreign student can't legally access | No way to execute it, and returns are measured in USD |
| Any non-USD idea that ignores currency | Rules: performance is in USD, so FX risk must be addressed |

### 2. Strategies: Hard (our choice, amateur scope)

We pitch **one clear, long-only position with a thesis**. We do not pitch a strategy, meaning a process or set of rules that decides trades.

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Systematic / quant rules (factor tilts, momentum, sector rotation, signals that trigger trades) | Needs backtesting skill and robustness checks. Meli and Knudsen are experts and will spot overfitting quickly |
| Relative value / pairs / "long A, short B" structures | Needs shorting (banned), and a long-only substitute is a strategy anyway |
| Multi-position baskets or model portfolios that need rebalancing | This is a strategy, not a trade idea, and it's hard to defend on every line |
| Market timing / trading around a position / tactical in-and-out | Rosenwald dislikes short-termism, and we have no execution edge |
| Merger-arb spreads | Dinan built a career on this. We can't out-judge him on deal risk |
| Anything whose pitch depends on the backtest | Knudsen: backward-looking data is "the impetus to ask questions but not the basis" for a decision |

Tradeoff: this gives up the **$10K data-driven/quant prize** as a target. The **$10K macro/fixed-income prize** is still open through a single bond or bond-ETF position. Alternative data that *supports* a single idea is still fine (see Meli's CarMax case). What we avoid is a rules-based system.

### 3. Instruments that are legal but toxic: Hard for us

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Leveraged ETFs (2x/3x) and inverse/short ETFs | Allowed, but the rules call out their risks. Daily reset decay is hard to defend. Meli distrusts casual leverage, and Knudsen asks about embedded leverage |
| Futures-based ETFs (VIX products, USO-style oil, most commodity ETFs) | Contango/roll decay, and the fund holds derivatives inside it. BWET (freight futures) did make the 2025 finals, but only because the pitch made the mechanics the thesis. That's too far for us |
| Options-overlay ETFs (covered-call / "income", buffered/defined-outcome) | They are derivatives inside an ETF: an edge case for disqualification, and the payoff is hard to explain |
| Spot crypto ETFs (IBIT, ETHA, etc.) | Meli: "I'm not sure how traditional asset pricing even applies to Bitcoin." No cash flows to value |
| SPACs and de-SPACs | Dinan got burned (Enovix, ScanTech, Iconic). Usually no valuation floor |
| Illiquid micro-caps / thinly traded bonds | Dinan: "the scariest positions are the ones you can't get out of." Meli's first-round graders ask whether a ~$3mm student fund (MPSIF) could actually hold it |

### 4. Asset profiles the judges dislike: Hard

| Avoid | Judges it runs into |
|---|---|
| **Pre-revenue narrative stocks** (pure-play fusion, quantum, SMR/nuclear startups, humanoid-robot pure-plays, eVTOL) | Meli: 60-100x multiples are "at serious risk" if growth doesn't show up. Rosenwald wants a ~50% discount to intrinsic value. Dinan: "hope is not a strategy" |
| **Meme / retail-momentum stocks** | Meli calls them a "classic" bubble. Luxor's short triggers include "short-term market euphoria for a sector" |
| **Consensus mega-cap AI longs** (NVDA, MSFT, the Mag-7) | No variant view, and Dinan says the edge is judgment, not information. Dalton already owns NVDA, so it's derivative for Rosenwald |
| **Pure commodity-direction calls** ("war means oil goes up", "gold goes up") | Dinan hedges energy beta out on purpose and lost heavily on energy in 2018-20. Luxor won't invest where returns need "broader market conditions" |
| **Upstream oil & gas / E&P** | Dinan's scar tissue (above). NYU, Knudsen's endowment, divested from fossil-fuel extraction |
| **"The Fed will cut, so buy X" macro calls** | Meli: interest rates are "largely a sideshow"; he rejects trades that rely on an official put |
| **Yield stories resting on private-credit "low volatility"** (BDC yield pitches) | Meli: private credit's low volatility is "more a question of optics" |
| **Value traps / "zombies"**: cheap, but no catalyst and no aligned management | Rosenwald's syllabus teaches "Cannibals vs. Zombies". Every judge wants a named catalyst |

### 6. Market conditions: Soft (your "saturated / deflated / inflated" rule, made concrete)

| Avoid | Test |
|---|---|
| **Saturated**: the theme is already on every sell-side note and ETF | Many thematic ETFs launched in the last 12 months; top names held by most hedge funds (13F crowding) |
| **Inflated**: price already reflects the bull case | Multiples far above history and peers, with no earnings to back them (Meli's warning about rethinking "decades-old valuation metrics just to make sense of current prices") |
| **Deflated with no floor**: cheap because it's broken | Falling revenue, weak balance sheet, no catalyst, no insider buying. Note that deflated *with* a catalyst and a floor is exactly what Rosenwald and Dinan want, so we keep that case |

## Flags on the current brainstorm vectors

| Vector | What to avoid inside it | What survives the filter |
|---|---|---|
| War / political tension | Defense-stock momentum, oil or gold direction calls, sanctioned assets. The 2026 Iran conflict is already priced | Specific mispriced names hit by second-order effects (supply-chain relocation, reshoring, insurers, shipping routes) with a floor and a catalyst |
| AI solving hard equations | Hype pure-plays, and consensus AI mega-caps | Knudsen's "babies thrown out with the bathwater": cash-flowing incumbents wrongly sold as AI losers, or owner-operated suppliers at reasonable multiples (Dalton's own framing) |
| AI & Robotics | Humanoid-robot pure-plays, pre-revenue names | Profitable component or automation suppliers, possibly non-US (Japan/Korea/Taiwan fits Rosenwald), with FX addressed |
| Energy | Upstream E&P, oil-price calls, LNG names the judges own | Power/grid or infrastructure cash flows with a specific event, or credit (Dinan: "good businesses with bad balance sheets") |
| Fusion? | Every listed pure-play (pre-revenue) | Only profitable suppliers where fusion is optional upside, not the thesis |
| Quantum? | IONQ/RGTI/QBTS-type pure-plays | Same as fusion: only as a free option inside a business that is already cash-flowing |
