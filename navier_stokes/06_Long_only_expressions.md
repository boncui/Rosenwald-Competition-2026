## The one thesis-grade idea: RELX

**Variant view:** the market priced a *Legal*-segment fear onto a business that is 28.3% STM and
whose STM division is a **net beneficiary** of cheap AI writing.

- **Mechanics.** Under gold open access the publisher is paid per *accepted* article, so cheaper
  manuscript production raises units sold. FY2025 submissions **+20%**, output **+10%**; Taylor &
  Francis submissions +20%; Springer Nature articles +12%. STM is £2,714m revenue, £1,035m AOP,
  **38.1% margin — the group's highest.**
- **The fear was tested and failed.** In the half immediately after the de-rating, Legal grew
  **+10% revenue / +13% profit, accelerating.** Group margin rose 34.8% → 35.5%.
- **Provenance passes.** PE by FY-end: 31.5 → 26.7 → 32.9 → **34.8 (FY24 peak)** → 26.6 → **19.45**.
  EV/EBITDA 24.3 → **16.03, 34% below peak and below every year in the series.** The multiple was at
  a five-year *high* eighteen months ago and broke in two dated steps. This does **not** predate the
  AI story — the trap that killed FactSet.
- **Natural experiment.** Thirty years of arXiv, PMC, Sci-Hub and Plan S, and STM's H1 margin went
  **35.8% (2019) → 37.9% (2026)**. RELX was never selling scarce content; it sells certification
  and the bundled subscription.
- **Quality.** 99% cash conversion, 7%/9% underlying growth, £2.25bn buyback, **beta 0.26**.

**Where a bear attacks, named rather than left for a judge to find:**
1. **Databases, Tools & Electronic Reference — ~40% of divisional revenue** per management on the H1
   call. Scopus and ScienceDirect are *retrieval* products, which is precisely what a model with tool
   access does. **RELX does not disclose the Primary Research vs Databases split, and B2 refused to
   construct one.** This is the real risk and it cannot be sized from filings.
2. **Gross Jevons, net unknown.** Submissions +20% vs output +10% implies a rising rejection rate.
   Screening cost is borne on submissions, revenue earned on acceptances, and no publisher discloses
   screening cost.
3. **AI doubled an existing trend, it did not create one.** Scholarly output has compounded at
   4–6.5% since long before ChatGPT.
4. **FX is live.** GBP reporter, USD scoring; currency cut H1 revenue growth by 2pp.

## Plain-language close

A team of AI agents proved something true and hard about fluid equations, and almost nobody who
works with fluids for a living cares. The proof describes a swirl about the width of the gap between
air molecules, far smaller than anything an engineer models, and the equations engineers actually use
never depended on the question it settled. We looked for companies this hurts and found that the
stock market agrees with the engineers: nothing moved. Aeroplane-engine makers sit near record highs,
and the software firms that sell simulation had their best year.

What we did find, looking sideways, is that investors panicked about a *different* AI story seven
months earlier — when Anthropic taught its assistant to do legal work, and everything that sells
information for a living fell about 10% in a single day. Some of those businesses genuinely are at
risk. At least one, the scientific publisher RELX, probably is not: it gets paid for each paper it
accepts, so cheap AI writing sends it more business, and it has survived thirty years of people
giving away free alternatives with its margins higher at the end than the beginning.

So: the honest answer to the question we were asked is that this breakthrough harms no one we can
buy. The idea worth keeping came from noticing which fear the market actually acted on — and that is
a different pitch, which should be argued on its own evidence rather than dressed up as this one.
